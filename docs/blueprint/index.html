Learn any move
from one take.

Simon lets a trainer record a movement once. Anyone can then follow it in front of a phone camera, and Simon compares the two bodies in 3D and says what differed, when, and by how much.

02 · Product

The problem and the idea

Form feedback today comes from a person watching you, or from apps with hand-written joint rules for each exercise they support. A trainer can't teach a new movement to such an app, and a physio can't hand a patient their own routine. Simon learns the movement from one recording, so any routine (a lift, a dance phrase, a rehab exercise) becomes something a follower can be coached on.

Who it is for

Trainer

Records once, coaches many

A personal trainer or instructor who wants clients to train between sessions with the same cues they would give in person.

Follower

Gets a coach at home

Someone following a routine from a phone, at any angle and in any room, who wants to know what to fix, not just a rep count.

Physio

Prescribes a movement

Records a patient's exercise in the clinic; the patient follows it at home, and the physio sees how close each session was.

Studio

Scores the whole room

A dance or group-fitness studio scoring a class from one phone, and running challenges between members.

05 · Scenarios

Scenarios

Nine main flows and five failure flows. Each names the outcome the user should see.

Trainer records a routine

S1
  1. Opens Record, sees the camera preview, starts a 3 s countdown.
  2. Performs 8 lateral raises, trims the dead time.
  3. Parts 1–4 find the reps, build the 3D skeleton and the reference animation.
  4. Names it; it appears in the Exercise library.

Outcome: a routine anyone can follow, with no rules written for lateral raises.

Follower trains at home

S2
  1. Picks the routine, props the phone at a side angle.
  2. Gets a pre-record tip: "Turn on a light in front of you".
  3. Follows along; the live check flags the right elbow.
  4. Waits for the 3D comparison while the lime paws walk; about 40 s on the serverless route, longer on the host.

Outcome: the score bar fills to 11,661 points (49%, E at Standard; D at Lenient, F at Strict); pink pops at each miss say what to fix; "What to work on" lists the top 3 cues; the worst rep replays side by side.

Dancer learns a phrase

S3
  1. Switches to Dance and opens a salsa basic.
  2. Follows the trainer's video, mirrored.
  3. Part 5 scores shape; timing is coached but never lowers the score.

Outcome: "Keep finding your rhythm", Shape 82, Timing "Rushing", and a drift track of the moments to fix.

Patient does a rehab exercise

S4
  1. The physio records a knee extension in the clinic.
  2. The patient follows it daily from the Rehab tab.
  3. The result uses gentle wording; progress shows over sessions.

Outcome: the patient knows whether today's range matched; the physio sees the trend.

Studio scores a class

S5
  1. The instructor films the room from one phone.
  2. Part 2 tracks each person with a stable id.
  3. Picks people from the crops; each gets a 3D body and a score.

Outcome: one take, a score per member, each row saying why a person is capped. Run on licensed group clips (Pexels, MM-Fit); not yet on a real class.

Friends compete

S6
  1. After a take, taps "Challenge a friend" and enters a board name.
  2. Shares the link; friends follow the same routine.
  3. The board ranks them until the end date.

Outcome: a reason to come back, and a free acquisition loop.

Dark room

F1
  1. The follower records in poor light.
  2. The quality check names the issue with a time and a tip.

Outcome: a warning names the light issue with a tip and Record again. The score is not capped: it reads as the credit with the warning beside it.

Wrong movement

F2
  1. The follower performs a lunge against a lateral-raise routine.
  2. Part 5's same-movement guard reports a low match.

Outcome: still analysed and flagged low_match, with a warning that says so (no "capped" claim); the credit shows what did match.

Meta key refused

F3
  1. The SAM 3.1 call is refused (expired or rotated key).
  2. Part 7 retries the people step with the local tracker (SAM 2.1 + YOLOX).

Outcome: the take completes; steps/part7.log records the fallback.

Student uses their own rep

S7
  1. A first take is scored against the teacher, and says a low score is not by itself a mistake.
  2. The student marks their best rep as "My reference", or the teacher approves it.
  3. The next take says "Compared with your own reference rep".

Outcome: the comparison that works on video: AUC 0.70 on labelled rehab reps, against chance for another person's rep.

Brings their own character

S8
  1. Opens My 3D model and imports an FBX, or a picture of a character.
  2. Simon rigs it (SAM 3D Objects, SAM 3D Body, MHR) with progress steps, about 5 minutes warm for a picture.
  3. Sets it as default; every result replays with it.

Outcome: the take replayed on their own body or character; a model whose hands touch its thighs can show stretched skin until release 22's repair.

Sees themselves doing it right

S9
  1. Opens a miss on the result.
  2. Presses "You, doing it right".
  3. Muse Image redraws their own frame with the joint moved; Simon re-measures it.

Outcome: their own picture in the corrected pose in about 25 s, or nothing when the drawing missed the target.

GPU route fails

F4
  1. Part 2 fails on its route: a crash, a timeout, or Modal unreachable.
  2. With the failsafe on, the same step reruns once on the other route.

Outcome: the take completes; part2.fallback.log and a p2_route_fallback entry record it (release 22).

A dance take of a different length

F5
  1. A follower dances 15 to 22 s of a 60 s reference.
  2. Part 5's alignment accepts only half to twice the trainer's speed.

Outcome: low_match and no credit score. A search for the matching section is proposed, not built.

06 · Architecture

The parts meet only through JSON files in the job's folder. Only Part 2 needs a GPU.

09 · Scoring

How a take is scored

Each rep, or each 5 s window when there are no reps, is scored on its own misses, and the take's score is their mean. One miss in a minute costs about what ten misses in ten minutes cost, so long and short takes compare fairly.

Fixed (#59). Persistence was measured over the whole clip (38%). On a 10 s multi-rep take, a 1.6 s, 20° shallower hinge was dropped, and the take scored 100 with 0 faults. The fix (ADR-0017) measures persistence per rep and compares rep by rep whenever the trainer has two or more reps. Published on Part 5 3530514 (1 Nov): the take now reports trunk_lean_forward 16.8° less than the trainer in the first of two reps, and scores 93 (A). The ruling (TICKET-047): a take that reports a fault should not grade A. simon.score.v2 (each miss weighted by how much the trainer moves that measure; any fault capped at 89) failed its held-out check on score seed 59, so v1 stayed and this take is a recorded A. On the operator's ruling to reconcile, simon.score.v3 keeps v2's improvements: one reported fault caps a take at 89 (B), two or more at 79, and the length-agnostic mean survives as score.uncapped. v3 passed its one held-out score on seed 61 and shipped in Part 5 1.11.0 (TICKET-060, ADR-0019). Detection passed its held-out checks: attempt 5 passed seed 71, was confirmed on seed 73, and make check-5 exits 0 (Part 5 e077f44).

Percent or points (simon.credit.v1). The penalty score drops to 0 for a beginner with about six faults per rep, so Part 5 adds a credit score next to it, in the style of a dance game. Every frame, each measure earns credit by how close it is: within half its tolerance 1, within the tolerance 0.6, within twice the tolerance 0.2, otherwise 0. The percentage is points over maximum, weighted by time. Points give 100 per measure per second, counted on the trainer's clock so one routine has one maximum (a half-speed take no longer doubles its points), and the level scales both: Easy (tolerance ×1.5, points ×0.75), Normal, and Hard (tolerance ×0.7, points ×1.5). The credit has its own grades: S 95, A 85, B 75, C 65, D 55, E 45, F 30, and under 30 still F. They never change score.grade. The app shows points by default, with a switch to %. score.credit is null when nothing was compared.

What the credit score showed. On real dance follows (docs/research/dance-scoring-2026-11/), the old score gave 16 of 25 single takes a 0. The 19 the credit could compare spread from 35% to 56%, mostly E. A group dancer following the leader in the same clip reached 86.8% (A). After the published Part 5 the operator's takes read, for example, 11,661 of 23,760 points (49.1%, E) and 20,056 of 21,560 (93.0%, A).

Whose rep is the reference. On physiotherapist-labelled rehab reps (REHAB24-6, pre-registered), a person's own correct rep separates their correct reps from incorrect ones at AUC 0.70 (0.65 to 0.86) on video, and the correct take scored higher for 6 of 6 people (median +12.6 points). Another person's correct rep does no better than chance on video (AUC 0.43 and 0.394). That is why "My reference" exists, and why a low score against someone else's rep is not by itself an error. The mistakes in that data were acted, and nobody got a cue between takes.

Nothing capped (team, 2 Nov). A warning (low match, poor video, measures left out) is shown in a banner but never lowers the score or the grade. The old cap of 59 still exists in score.value, which the app no longer shows on any take that has a credit; only analyses older than 1.13.0 fall back to the old 0 to 100 score with A to F.

Seated and upper-body routines (Part 5 1.14.2, #38). A measure the trainer's own clip can't show is not "left out" of the take, so a seated, chest-up routine no longer reads quality "major". The first case was b5acba462f16: 99 A, 0 faults, all 10 moving measures compared, but 15 of 22 measures counted as hidden. The last second of a take, getting out of the pose, also stops costing credit. Re-run on 1.14.2, the same recording reads quality ok and 3,191 of 3,500 points, 91.2%, A (was 82.9%, B).

Quality. Part 5's quality block marks a take ok, degraded or major (light, dropout, heading offset past 5°) and reports a cap of 59 when major. A dark video whose pose files carry full confidence still reads ok, because Part 5 sees no light signal in the skeleton.

Different-length takes. Part 5's time alignment accepts a follower between half and twice the trainer's speed, so a 15–22 s dance clip against a 60 s reference is not compared. A sliding search for the matching section of the reference is proposed and not built.

Live check. During the take, MediaPipe on the phone draws the joints, says whether the whole body is in view, and compares 8 joint angles with the trainer's animation, flagging joints more than 25° off. It is labelled approximate; the 3D score after the take is the one that counts.

What the model assumes

  • 65 takes per active user a month (the plan's figure) and 15% of a day's takes in the busiest hour.
  • Measured 37 s of GPU per 10 s take, warm; serverless adds 15% for cold starts and warm windows at scale.
  • Modal L40S at $1.95 an hour on demand. A rented always-on L40S at $0.74 an hour, a planning price. An always-on GPU runs at most 70% busy at peak.
  • An always-on GPU pays off over serverless once it is busy more than about 33% of the time ($0.74 ÷ $1.95 = 38%, adjusted for the 15% serverless overhead). The hybrid keeps that many on (average load ÷ 70%) and sends the rest to serverless.
  • Not modelled: GPU quotas and burst limits at large scale, owned hardware (an RTX 5090 bought outright; no host measurement at 3 fps / 5 fps backs a per-take figure yet), and longer takes (GPU time grows with clip length).

What the 2 November tests change

  • The base figure stands. 37 s of L40S time per 10 s take (2¢ in a busy stream) is still the only measured Modal figure. No Modal run has happened since 1 Nov, and release 22 adds the route to staging.
  • The host's own settings cost more per take. Staging runs 10 fps with the body mesh. Its pose step took 135 s with the warm worker and 397 s without it, and finding the people took about 70 s when it waited for the warm tracker, against about 150 s when it did not (2 Nov, takes of varying length). The table above assumes the serverless settings (people at 3 fps, 3D at 5 fps). If the product keeps the host's settings, the always-on and hybrid rows grow with the extra GPU time, so the settings choice matters more than the serving choice.
  • New one-off cost: your own 3D model. A picture becomes a textured, rigged model in 302 s warm and 712 s cold, on about 20 GB of GPU memory. At the L40S list price that is about $0.16 to $0.39 a model. It's a planning figure, measured on Mo's GPU rather than on Modal. It is paid once per user, not per take.
  • Voice licence. The spoken cues use Meta MMS-TTS, licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial). It's fine for the demo, but the paid plans need a commercially licensed voice or the voice limited to free use.
  • New per-use cost not yet measured: Muse Image ("You, doing it right", about 25 s a picture). Until it is priced, keep it on paid plans, as with Muse Spark phrasing.
  • Waiting is a cost too. On the shared host, takes waited 1,332 s per hour for the GPU before the yield, and 565 s after it. One always-on GPU serving takes and model jobs needs the yield, or the hybrid's serverless share has to absorb model jobs.
  • Value evidence. The student's own rep as the reference separates correct from incorrect reps (AUC 0.70 on labelled rehab video), and another person's rep does not. So the trainer's value is approving each student's reference, then Simon checking every later take. That fits the per-follower Trainer and Clinic plans as priced. The "one recording serves many students" pitch needs pose accuracy first. On staging today, one recording served at most 2 takes, so usage per recording is unproven.

Go to market

  1. Pilot (Nov–Dec 2026): 3 trainers, 1 dance studio, 1 physio clinic; free in exchange for weekly feedback and permission to measure accuracy on real takes.
  2. Trainer-led growth: every published routine is a link; followers arrive through their trainer, and challenges spread between followers.
  3. Studios and clinics: sold directly, with group scoring and adherence reports as the reason to upgrade.
  4. Sponsored challenges: brands pay for a board; followers get a prize; Simon gets reach.
