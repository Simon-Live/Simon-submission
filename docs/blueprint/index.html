One take makes
a coach.

Simon lets a trainer record a movement once. Anyone can then follow it in front of a phone camera, and Simon compares the two bodies in 3D and says what differed, when, and by how much.

01 · Summary

Where Simon stands

The whole journey works end to end: record a trainer, follow the movement, and get a 3D comparison, timed coaching and a score. Seven parts in seven repos are stitched by Part 7 into one web app. Only one step needs a GPU, and it runs either on a staging host or serverless on Modal.

7parts, each a separate repo with its own done-check
~3 minfor a 10 s take, upload to result, on the serverless route
$0.14per take with Meta APIs; about $0.07 on our own GPUs
14 / 16check-7 gates green on the serverless route (31 Oct)

The one thing that matters before the demo: scoring is too lenient on multi-rep takes. A take with a visibly shallower hinge scored 100 with 0 faults (#59). Mo has shipped near-miss diagnostics; the detection fix is in progress.

02 · Product

The problem and the idea

Form feedback today comes from a person watching you, or from apps with hand-written joint rules for each exercise they support. A trainer can't teach a new movement to such an app, and a physio can't hand a patient their own routine. Simon learns the movement from one recording, so any routine (a lift, a dance phrase, a rehab exercise) becomes something a follower can be coached on.

Who it is for

Trainer

Records once, coaches many

A personal trainer or instructor who wants clients to train between sessions with the same cues they would give in person.

Follower

Gets a coach at home

Someone following a routine from a phone, at any angle and in any room, who wants to know what to fix, not just a rep count.

Physio

Prescribes a movement

Records a patient's exercise in the clinic; the patient follows it at home, and the physio sees how close each session was.

Studio

Scores the whole room

A dance or group-fitness studio scoring a class from one phone, and running challenges between members.

05 · Scenarios

Scenarios

Six main flows and three failure flows. Each names the outcome the user should see.

Trainer records a routine

S1
  1. Opens Record, sees the camera preview, starts a 3 s countdown.
  2. Performs 8 lateral raises, trims the dead time.
  3. Parts 1–4 find the reps, build the 3D skeleton and the reference animation.
  4. Names it; it appears in the Exercise library.

Outcome: a routine anyone can follow, with no rules written for lateral raises.

Follower trains at home

S2
  1. Picks the routine, props the phone at a side angle.
  2. Gets a pre-record tip: "Turn on a light in front of you".
  3. Follows along; the live check flags the right elbow.
  4. Waits about 3 minutes for the 3D comparison.

Outcome: 77 / C; "rep 4 of 8" is the worst moment, shown as trainer vs you; tap to replay it.

Dancer learns a phrase

S3
  1. Switches to Dance and opens a salsa basic.
  2. Follows the trainer's video, mirrored.
  3. Part 5 scores shape; timing is coached but never lowers the score.

Outcome: "Keep finding your rhythm", Shape 82, Timing "Rushing", and a drift track of the moments to fix.

Patient does a rehab exercise

S4
  1. The physio records a knee extension in the clinic.
  2. The patient follows it daily from the Rehab tab.
  3. The result uses gentle wording; progress shows over sessions.

Outcome: the patient knows whether today's range matched; the physio sees the trend.

Studio scores a class

S5
  1. The instructor films the room from one phone.
  2. Part 2 tracks each person with a stable id.
  3. Picks people from the crops; each gets a 3D body and a score.

Outcome: one take, a score per member. Not yet tested on a real class.

Friends compete

S6
  1. After a take, taps "Challenge a friend" and enters a board name.
  2. Shares the link; friends follow the same routine.
  3. The board ranks them until the end date.

Outcome: a reason to come back, and a free acquisition loop.

Dark room

F1
  1. The follower records in poor light.
  2. The quality check names the issue with a time and a tip.

Outcome: a warning banner, the cap line "Capped at 59 because of the video quality", and Record again.

Wrong movement

F2
  1. The follower performs a lunge against a lateral-raise routine.
  2. Part 5's same-movement guard reports a low match.

Outcome: still analysed, flagged low_match, graded F. check-7's mismatch clip scores 0.

Meta key refused

F3
  1. The SAM 3.1 call is refused (expired or rotated key).
  2. Part 7 retries the people step with the local tracker (SAM 2.1 + YOLOX).

Outcome: the take completes; steps/part7.log records the fallback.

Shared conventions: 17 joints (nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) named by the subject's own left and right; origin at the hip midpoint; x to the subject's left, y up, z forward; metres and degrees.

09 · Scoring

How a take is scored

Each rep, or each 5 s window when there are no reps, is scored on its own misses, and the take's score is their mean. One miss in a minute costs about what ten misses in ten minutes cost, so long and short takes compare fairly.

Known gap (#59). Persistence was measured over the whole clip (38%). On a 10 s multi-rep take, a 1.6 s, 20° shallower hinge was dropped, and the take scored 100 with 0 faults. The fix (ADR-0017) measures persistence per rep and compares rep by rep whenever the trainer has two or more reps. Mo has shipped diagnostics.near_misses; detection is next.

Live check. During the take, MediaPipe on the phone draws the joints, says whether the whole body is in view, and compares 8 joint angles with the trainer's animation, flagging joints more than 25° off. It is labelled approximate; the 3D score after the take is the one that counts.

742 Python and 158 JS tests in Part 7; Playwright suites for the result, coach, challenge, people and quality screens.

Two Part 2 runs of the same clip on Modal give slightly different 3D. Next step: deterministic GPU settings on the serverless branch. The quality cap waits on Parts 2, 3 and 5.

Security

  • MODEL_API_KEY lives only in .env files, the operator key folder and the Modal secret; never in a repo, an image or a log.
  • The Part 7 server binds to localhost; a tunnel exposes it for testing, with daily job caps on.
  • Issue and comment text is treated as data: no agent changes secrets, workflow permissions or repo settings because a comment asks.
  • Videos stay in the job folder of the machine that ran them; the serverless route sends the clip to Modal for the Part 2 call only.
13 · Business plan

Business plan

Simon sells to the people who already own the movement: trainers, studios and clinics. Their followers are the users; the professional pays, because Simon lets one recording do the work of many sessions.

All figures here are planning estimates. Costs come from measured runs and 2026 list prices; prices and conversion rates are assumptions to test in the pilot.

At 10,000 active users a month: ~$92,000 compute with the Meta APIs, $35,000 to $48,000 on rented GPUs. Serverless is cheaper until a rented GPU would be busy more than ~38% of the time; from about 1,000 active users, an always-on base plus serverless peaks is cheapest. Hybrid here means ~$0.054 a take ($3.50 for 65 takes). The Trainer plan stays thin even then, so its follower allowance or price is the first thing the pilot should test.

Go to market

  1. Pilot (Nov–Dec 2026): 3 trainers, 1 dance studio, 1 physio clinic; free in exchange for weekly feedback and permission to measure accuracy on real takes.
  2. Trainer-led growth: every published routine is a link; followers arrive through their trainer, and challenges spread between followers.
  3. Studios and clinics: sold directly, with group scoring and adherence reports as the reason to upgrade.
  4. Sponsored challenges: brands pay for a board; followers get a prize; Simon gets reach.
